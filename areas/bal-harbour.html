Why Bal Harbour Needs It

Highest Exposure,
Highest Standard

Bal Harbour is as exposed as South Florida gets - ocean on one side, Intracoastal on the other, and almost nothing in between to break the salt. Surfaces here accumulate coastal film faster than anywhere inland, and because the property values and finishes are what they are, the margin for error on method is small. Travertine, marble, polished stone and high-end paint are all easy to ruin with the wrong pressure or the wrong chemistry.

South Florida Climate

Miami-Dade and Broward average over 60 inches of rain per year. Humidity and warmth keep algae, mold, and mildew growing on exterior surfaces year-round.

Finish Quality Matters

Acidic cleaners must never touch marble, limestone or coral stone - they etch it chemically, and that damage is permanent. A contractor using one general-purpose cleaner on every surface is a genuine risk on property finished to this level.

Surface Types

Marble and travertine terraces, polished stone entries, painted and rendered building exteriors, glass railings and frames, motor courts and pool surrounds. Low pressure and pH-neutral solution throughout.

Pressure washing in Bal Harbour: what's different here

Bal Harbour is a small village at the northern tip of Miami Beach, between the Atlantic and Biscayne Bay, with Haulover Inlet just to the north. Collins Avenue runs through the middle. Almost everything here is within sight of the water, so salt film is part of owning a building or home.

The surfaces are also less forgiving than usual: marble, travertine, polished stone, glass and high-end paint. A cleaner that works on a plain driveway can ruin them. The method has to match the material, every time.
