How to Clean a Travertine Pool Deck Without Etching It

Travertine makes a beautiful pool deck and a fragile one. It is softer and far more porous than concrete, which means the cleaning methods that work on a driveway will permanently damage it. Here is how to get it clean without wrecking it.

The two ways travertine gets ruined

Too much pressure. Blast travertine with a high-pressure tip and you etch it. The surface goes pitted and rough, and no amount of future cleaning smooths it back out. The damage is permanent.

Acidic cleaners. This is the one people do not see coming. Acids etch travertine, marble, coral stone, and limestone on a chemical level. A contractor reaching for a strong acid-based cleaner to speed things up will leave dull, eaten patches in the stone. On natural stone, acid is never the answer.

The safe way

Travertine is cleaned with controlled low pressure and a surface cleaner kept moving, paired with a pH-appropriate solution that lifts the algae and grime without attacking the stone. The goal is to kill the growth and rinse the dirt, not to sandblast the surface. Done right, the deck comes back clean and keeps its texture.

Because travertine is porous, sealing it after cleaning is worth considering. A penetrating sealer slows down staining and algae and makes the next cleaning easier, without the slippery film a glossy sealer can leave around a pool.

Why the slippery deck is a safety issue

A green travertine deck is not just ugly, it is genuinely slick when wet, and it is the most underestimated hazard around a South Florida pool. The fix is killing the algae, which is exactly what a proper clean does. Here is how we handle travertine and natural stone.

Common Questions

Can you pressure wash travertine?
Only at controlled low pressure with the cleaner kept moving. Travertine is softer and more porous than concrete and etches permanently under aggressive pressure, leaving a pitted texture that cannot be fixed by more cleaning.

What should never go on travertine?
Acidic cleaners. Acids etch travertine, marble, coral stone, and limestone chemically, and that damage is not recoverable. Anything with muriatic or similar acid stays off natural stone, full stop.

Why is my travertine deck slippery?
Almost always algae, fed by splash-out, shade from screen enclosures, and constant foot traffic. Killing the algae with a proper clean is what makes the deck safe again, not just rinsing the surface.
