Updated November 2026

Pressure washing in Miami-Dade costs from $119 for a driveway to $349 for a small roof at SoFlo UnderPressure. Those are starting prices for a small, lightly dirty surface at our standard Miami rate. Your number depends on size, buildup, access, and your area, and the instant quote shows it by address. Published prices for the same job vary widely, so get a flat price for your own property before you decide.

What changes a quote

Size. More surface takes more time and more product. In our quote engine a medium driveway, patio, house or roof is priced at 1.5 times the small price, a large one at 2.1 times, and an extra-large one at 2.8 times. Walkways and fences scale up more slowly.

Buildup. Moderate buildup adds 25 percent and heavy buildup adds 55 percent. A roof that has gone fully green and black takes more product and time than one with light streaking. Heavy oil or rust stains add $50 on a first clean.

Access. Two-story homes cost 30 percent more for the house wash and 15 percent more for the roof. Tight side yards, screened enclosures, and landscaping crowding the walls add time too, and we factor that in when we confirm your flat price.

Tile roof. Tile roofs cost 30 percent more than the shingle price. The roof soft washing cost guide shows examples by zone.

Surface type. A plain concrete driveway and a travertine pool deck are not the same job. Travertine, barrel tile, and painted stucco need a gentler, slower method, and that costs more than blasting concrete with a surface cleaner.

Bundling lowers the per-surface cost

Book more than one surface in one visit and the extra surfaces are priced at 60 percent, with the most expensive surface at full price. As an example from our quote engine, a small driveway ($119) plus a small house soft wash ($279) at the standard rate comes to about $350 instead of $398. That is an example, not a quote. If you are on a recurring plan, the per-visit price drops again, since the property never gets bad enough to need a heavy restoration clean.

The quoting tricks worth knowing

Two patterns to watch for. The first is the suspiciously low phone number that climbs once the crew is standing in your driveway. The second is a single whole-property price from someone who never asked what your surfaces are made of. Travertine, tile, stucco, and concrete need four different approaches. The person quoting should want to know which they are dealing with.

The honest version is a flat price, given up front, after someone has actually looked at the property or clear photos of it. That is how we quote every job, and it is why the number on the estimate is the number on the invoice.

Common questions

Why won't a company give a price over the phone?
A real quote depends on surface type, square footage, and how bad the growth is. Anyone who quotes a flat number sight-unseen is either padding it to cover the unknown or planning to raise it on the day. We quote from the address, measurements, or a few photos so the number holds.

Is pressure washing priced by the hour or the job?
Good local companies price the job, not the hour. Hourly billing rewards slow work. A flat per-job price means you know the cost before anyone shows up, and the crew has a reason to work efficiently.

Does a bigger house always cost more?
Mostly, but square footage is only part of it. A smaller house with heavy roof algae and a screened enclosure can cost more than a larger, cleaner one. Surface type and condition move the price as much as size does.
