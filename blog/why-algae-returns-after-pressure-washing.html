Why Algae Comes Back After Pressure Washing

You paid for a pressure wash, the surface looked great for a month, and now the green is back. This is one of the most common complaints in South Florida, and the reason is simple once you know it.

Pressure washing treats the symptom, not the cause

The green and black on your roof, walls, and pavers is living growth: algae, mildew, and in shaded spots, mold. It has a root structure that sits down in the pores of the surface.

A pressure washer blasts off the top layer you can see. It looks clean immediately. But the roots are still down in the pores, untouched. In Miami's heat and year-round humidity, that root regrows fast, and often comes back worse, because the blasting opened up the surface and made it more porous than before.

Soft washing kills it at the root

Soft washing uses a cleaning solution that actually kills the organism, roots included, instead of just scraping off the visible layer. Nothing is left in the pores to regrow from. That is why a soft-washed surface stays clean for a year or two in the same conditions where a pressure wash lasts a couple of months.

This is the whole reason roofs, stucco, and shaded walls should be soft washed and not pressure washed. It is not about being gentle for its own sake. It is about actually solving the problem instead of resetting the clock for a few weeks.

What to do about it

If growth keeps coming back, the fix is not more pressure, it is the right method and a schedule. A recurring plan keeps surfaces from ever reaching the heavy-growth stage, which is cheaper over time than paying for a full restoration clean every year. Common Questions

Why does algae grow back so fast after pressure washing?
Pressure washing removes the surface layer you can see but leaves the root structure in the pores of the surface. In South Florida's heat and humidity it regrows from that root, often faster, because the surface is now more porous.

What actually keeps algae from coming back?
Soft washing, which uses a solution that kills the organism at the root rather than blasting off the visible layer. Killed growth takes far longer to return than growth that was only scraped off the surface.

How long should a proper clean last?
A soft-washed roof or wall in South Florida typically stays clean one to two years. A pressure-washed one can green up again within a few months, especially on shaded, north-facing surfaces.
