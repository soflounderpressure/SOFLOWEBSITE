What Salt Air Does to Your Home's Exterior Near the Coast

If you live within a few miles of the water in Miami Beach, Surfside, Sunny Isles, or along the coast, your home is dealing with something inland homes are not: constant salt in the air. It does more damage than most owners realize.

What salt actually does

Salt carried on the ocean breeze settles onto every exterior surface and does not just sit there. It is hygroscopic, meaning it pulls moisture out of the air and holds it against whatever it has landed on. That constant dampness speeds up three problems at once: corrosion on metal, buildup on walls and glass, and algae growth that feeds on the retained moisture.

Which surfaces suffer most

Screen enclosures and metal fixtures. Railings, frames, light fixtures, and hardware corrode faster in salt air. Removing the salt regularly slows that down.

Windows and glass. Salt film dulls glass and etches it over time if left to build.

Stucco and painted walls. Salt plus moisture equals faster algae and a grimier surface, especially on the sides facing the water.

Why regular cleaning is prevention, not vanity

You cannot stop living near the ocean from being salty, but you can keep the salt from sitting on the house for months at a time. Washing it off on a regular schedule removes the moisture-holding film before it accelerates corrosion and growth. For coastal homes, that usually means a shorter cleaning interval than inland properties need. A recurring plan is built for exactly this, and here is where we work along the coast.

Common Questions

Does salt air really affect my house?
Yes. Salt settles on every exterior surface near the coast, holds moisture against it, and accelerates corrosion on metal and buildup on walls, screens, and glass. Homes within a few miles of the water see it clearly.

How often should a coastal home be cleaned?
More often than an inland one. Coastal exteriors benefit from cleaning every six to twelve months, sooner for screen enclosures and metal fixtures that salt attacks fastest.

Can cleaning undo salt corrosion?
It stops the ongoing damage by removing the salt and grime, but corrosion already in metal cannot be reversed by cleaning. Regular removal is about prevention, which is why staying on a schedule matters near the water.
