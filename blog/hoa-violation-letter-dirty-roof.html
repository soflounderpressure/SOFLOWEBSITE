HOA Guide

Got an HOA Violation Letter for a Dirty Roof? What to Do

An HOA violation letter over a dirty roof or stained walls is stressful, usually time-limited, and completely fixable. Here is how to handle it without the fine.

First, read the letter for the deadline

Every association runs on its own timeline, but the letter will name a cure period, often somewhere between two and four weeks, before fines begin. Find that date first. It tells you how fast you need to move, and most cleaning can be scheduled comfortably inside that window if you do not sit on it.

Match the cleaning to what the letter names

Most of these letters are about one of three things: black streaks on the roof, algae on the walls, or a stained driveway and walkway. All three are living growth, and all three come off with the right method. Roof and walls get soft washed, the driveway gets pressure washed. Once the surface is back to clean, the violation is resolved.

Keep the invoice and a couple of before-and-after photos. If the association needs proof the work was done, you have it.

Then stop it from happening again

Here is the part most people skip. These letters almost always come because growth was left until it was obvious from the street. The fix is a schedule, not a one-time scramble. A recurring plan keeps the roof, walls, and driveway below the level that triggers a letter in the first place, which is cheaper and far less stressful than reacting to each notice.

If you manage a community rather than live in one, we also handle whole-property and HOA contracts on one schedule and one invoice. Either way, send the address through the quote form and we will get you compliant before the deadline.

Common Questions

How long do I have to fix an HOA cleaning violation?
It depends on your association, but most give a set window, often 14 to 30 days, before fines start. Read the letter for the exact deadline and cure period, then book the cleaning with time to spare.

Will cleaning actually satisfy the violation?
Yes, if it addresses what the letter names. A soft wash that removes the roof streaks or wall algae brings the surface back to compliance. Keep the before-and-after and the invoice in case you need to show the work was done.

How do I stop getting these letters?
Get on a cleaning schedule. Most violations happen because growth was left until it was obvious from the street. A recurring clean keeps surfaces below the threshold that triggers letters.
