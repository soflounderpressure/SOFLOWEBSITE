Why pavers need a gentler technique than concrete

Pavers are set in a bed of sand, and the narrow joints between them are filled with more sand. That joint sand is part of what keeps the pavers locked together. High pressure aimed at the joints can wash it out, which is why many cleaners use lower pressure and a surface cleaner on pavers instead of a handheld wand.

A surface cleaner is a round housing with spinning jets that rides flat over the surface. It covers a wide path at an even distance, so you avoid the stripes and uneven patches a wand can leave behind. We use one on flat paver areas for a clean, consistent look.

What builds up on pavers in South Florida

Algae and mildew. Humidity, afternoon rain and shade turn pavers green or black, especially along the edges, in the joints and in damp corners.

Rust and iron staining. Orange or brown marks can come from sprinkler water, metal furniture or planters.

Oil and tire marks. Common on paver driveways, especially near the garage door and where cars park.

White haze. Pavers can sometimes develop a chalky white film called efflorescence. It is a general possibility with concrete and brick pavers, and we look at it when we see it.

Will cleaning damage my pavers?

Pavers are more sensitive than poured concrete, which is why many cleaners avoid high pressure and a handheld wand on them. We use a surface cleaner for even results. If your pavers are old, loose or already crumbling, tell us when you ask for a quote so we can plan for it.

How often should pavers be cleaned?

Once or twice a year is common in South Florida. Pavers in shade, near landscaping or around a pool often need it more often. If they look green, dark or spotted, it is time.
