This One Is a Safety Issue

Most exterior cleaning is about appearance. Pool decks aren't. Algae on a wet deck is genuinely slippery, and a pool deck is wet by definition, usually with people walking on it barefoot.

It's the item homeowners most consistently underestimate. The deck doesn't look dangerous - it looks slightly dull - right up until someone goes down on it.

Why Pool Decks Grow Faster Than Anything Else

They get the worst combination in South Florida: constant moisture from splash-out, shade from screen enclosures and umbrellas, and foot traffic grinding organic matter into the surface. Add pool chemistry and you have close to ideal conditions for algae.

Travertine and Natural Stone - Read This Part

Travertine is everywhere on Miami pool decks, and it is softer and more porous than concrete. Aggressive pressure leaves permanent pitting and etching that no subsequent cleaning fixes. Once a travertine deck is etched, it stays etched.

It can be cleaned properly - with a surface cleaner at controlled low pressure, kept moving, never held in one place. The setting matters and so does the technique.

Chemistry matters just as much: acidic cleaners must never be used on travertine, marble, coral stone or limestone. They etch it chemically, which looks like a dull cloudy patch and is not recoverable. Plenty of contractors use a general-purpose acidic cleaner on everything. Ask.

If a contractor treats your travertine deck the same way they treat your concrete driveway, stop them.

Surfaces We Handle

Travertine, marble, coral and keystone - controlled low pressure, pH-neutral solution.

Pavers - cleaned then re-sanded.

Stamped and decorative concrete - reduced pressure to protect the color layer and sealer.

Poured concrete and lanais - full surface-cleaner treatment.

Screen enclosure frames - soft washed alongside, since they're right there and usually filthy.

How Often

Every 6 to 12 months. Shorter if the deck is screened, heavily shaded, or under trees. Natural stone also stains more readily than concrete, so letting a travertine deck go is more expensive to put right than letting concrete go.

Patio & Pool Deck Cleaning - FAQ

How much does pool deck cleaning cost in Miami?

Patio and pool deck cleaning starts at $129, quoted flat once we know the square footage and surface material. Bundling the deck with a driveway clean in the same visit is cheaper than booking two separate trips.

Can you pressure wash travertine without damaging it?

Yes, but only at controlled low pressure with a surface cleaner kept moving. Travertine is softer and more porous than concrete and etches permanently under aggressive pressure. Acidic cleaners must also be avoided entirely on travertine, marble, coral stone and limestone, because they etch the stone chemically and that damage is not recoverable.

Why is my pool deck slippery?

Almost always algae. Pool decks combine constant moisture from splash-out, shade from screen enclosures, and foot traffic, which is close to ideal for algae growth. It is genuinely slippery when wet and is the most underestimated safety issue on a South Florida property.

How often should a pool deck be cleaned in South Florida?

Every 6 to 12 months, and toward the shorter end if the deck is screened, shaded or under trees. Natural stone stains more readily than concrete, so a travertine deck left too long is more expensive to restore than a concrete one.

Do you clean screen enclosures too?

Yes. Enclosure frames and screens are soft washed at low pressure while we are on site, since they are usually the dirtiest thing around the deck and look far worse from the street than from inside.
