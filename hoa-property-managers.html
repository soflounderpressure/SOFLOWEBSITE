One Schedule, the Whole Community

Managing a community means the exterior is never really "done." Driveways streak, roofs green over, walls grow mildew, and it all happens at slightly different speeds across every unit. Handling each one as it comes up is how a property ends up looking uneven and how a manager ends up on the phone all month.

We put the whole community on a set cleaning cycle instead. Driveways, roofs, house and stucco exteriors, fences, patios and pool decks — cleaned on a recurring schedule so the property stays level and predictable, not patched one complaint at a time. Recurring service is the core of it, and our membership plans are built for exactly this cadence.

Stay Ahead of the Violation Letter

Exterior grime is the single most common trigger for HOA violation notices in South Florida — green roofs, black driveway stains, mildew on walls and walkways. When those show up, someone has to notice, notify, follow up and re-inspect. That is work nobody wants.

A schedule removes the trigger. If roofs and driveways are cleaned before the algae is visible from the street, the letters never get written. For boards, that means fewer disputes with owners. For managers, it means the property polices itself instead of you policing the property.

One Call, Every Common Area

The shared surfaces are usually where a community shows its wear first, because everyone uses them. We map them all into one scope: sidewalks and breezeways, pool decks, clubhouse exteriors, perimeter and entry walls, mailbox kiosks, dumpster enclosures and covered parking.

Common areas and individual units go on the same visit and the same plan, so nothing shared falls through the gap between "the association's job" and "the owner's job." One call covers all of it.

One Invoice, Not Twenty

Piecing a property together from separate vendors means separate schedules, separate quality, and a stack of invoices to reconcile. We quote the whole property as one job and bill it as one job. One scope, one price agreed up front, one invoice — clean paperwork for the board's books and for the manager's approvals.

The Right Method for Every Surface

A property is not one surface, and using the same high pressure on all of it causes damage. Roof tile, painted stucco, shingles and window screens need a gentle, low-pressure soft washing approach; concrete driveways and pavers take a proper pressure wash. Getting that wrong is how a "cleaning" turns into a repair bill.

We match method to material every time — soft washing where pressure would strip granules, crack tile or blast paint, and full pressure where the surface can take it. That protects the association's assets while still getting everything genuinely clean. Roof algae in particular needs the low-pressure treatment covered on our roof soft washing page.
